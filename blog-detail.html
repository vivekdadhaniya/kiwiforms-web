1. Kiwiform : best free quiz maker with no student cap

Our pick Unlimited free responses

Kiwiform is a quiz and form builder that doesn't limit students. The free plan includes unlimited quizzes and unlimited responses, so you can quiz a class of 25 or a grade of 400 without upgrading. Questions appear one at a time, which keeps students focused and works well on phones and Chromebooks.

What sets it apart for teaching is logic. Scoring and calculations, quiz variables and multiple endings are all free, so you can show different results by score. Branching can send a student who misses a question to a hint or a follow-up question. The AI quiz generator drafts questions, answer options and scoring from a prompt or your lesson notes, and you edit everything before sharing.

2. Google Forms: best free tool for simple graded quizzes

Quiz mode adds answer keys, point values and automatic grading, and it connects natively to Google Classroom and Sheets. In July 2026 Google added Gemini "Help me create" quiz generation, but only on eligible paid editions (such as Education Plus and Google AI Pro for Education).

Classroom example: a Friday vocabulary quiz assigned in Google Classroom, auto-graded, with scores imported into the gradebook.

  • Pros
  • Free and familiar
  • Google Classroom grade import
  • Cons
  • No live or gamified mode
  • Plain design; basic section-based logic
Google Forms alternatives

3. Kahoot!: best for live review games

Kahoot! turns quizzes into live games with music, timers and leaderboards, and assignments allow self-paced play too. The free plan caps games at 40 players. Kahoot!+ plans start at $3/month ($36/year) for 50 players and add AI creation from a topic, URL or file.

Classroom example: a 15-minute unit review the day before a test, with teams competing on the projector.

  • Pros
  • Unmatched classroom energy
  • Huge public quiz library
  • Cons
  • 40-player free cap
  • Speed-based scoring can favor fast guessers

4. Wayground (formerly Quizizz): best for homework & self-paced

Quizizz is now Wayground. The free Basic plan supports up to 100 students and 20 stored activities, with live and student-paced modes, AI content generation and AI answer explanations. Grade passback, Canvas/Schoology LTI and Clever/ClassLink rostering are on quote-based School/District plans.

  • Pros
  • Great self-paced homework mode
  • AI tools on the free plan
  • Cons
  • Only 20 activities stored on free
  • No public price for individual teachers

5. Blooket: best game-based quiz tool

Blooket wraps your question sets in a variety of game modes, and students love it. The free plan allows 60 players per live game and homework with deadlines up to 14 days. Blooket Plus (about $4.99/month billed yearly) unlocks more modes, enhanced reports and bigger games.

6. Gimkit: best strategy-style quiz games

Built by a student, Gimkit adds in-game economies and strategy. Gimkit Basic (free) has no player cap but only around 3 rotating game modes. Pro ($14.99/month or $59.88/year) unlocks all modes and homework assignments.

7. Formative: best for real-time formative assessment

Formative shows student work live as they answer, including drawings and show-your-work responses. The free plan is limited (6 basic question types and 14 days of data), and paid plans start at $249/year with AI assistance and LMS grade passback.

8. Nearpod: best for interactive lessons

Nearpod puts quizzes, polls and activities inside your slides, run live or student-paced. The free Silver plan allows 40 students per lesson. Gold ($159/year) raises that to 75 and adds AI Create.

9. Socrative: best for quick checks & exit tickets

Socrative is fast and simple, with quizzes, quick questions and Space Race. Free covers 5 quizzes, 1 room, 50 students per activity and 15 AI generations a week. Essentials costs $10/month ($120/year).

10. Microsoft Forms: for Microsoft 365 schools

It works like Google Forms for Microsoft schools, with auto-graded quizzes and Teams Assignments. Free personal accounts are capped at 200 responses per form, while education accounts get far higher limits. Copilot quiz drafting needs an eligible license.

11. Quizlet: best for flashcards & study practice

Quizlet is strongest for student self-study: flashcards, games and AI-generated practice tests. Teacher pricing has changed in 2026, so check Quizlet's teacher page for the current offer before you buy.

12. Mentimeter: best for live polls in lectures

Mentimeter is great for large lectures and PD sessions, with live quizzes and word clouds. The free plan allows 50 participants a month, and education plans are billed yearly.

Quiz makers vs test makers for teachers

Quiz makers are built for quick, frequent checks and engagement. Test makers are built for graded, formal assessment: question banks, timers, randomization and secure delivery. For unit tests and exams, Google Forms, Microsoft Forms and ClassMarker (credit-based pricing) are common choices. See our full guide to the best online test makers.

How to make an online quiz in 5 minutes (with Kiwiform)

1

Describe it. Open the AI quiz generator and type, for example, "10-question multiple-choice quiz on photosynthesis for grade 7, with answers and scoring."

2

Edit the questions. Fix wording, add images and set point values for each answer.

3

Add branching (optional). Send students who miss a key question to a hint or a follow-up question.

4

Set result endings. Show different ending screens by score range, such as "Mastered", "Review" or "Let's practice".

5

Share and review. Post the link in your LMS or class page, or embed it, then review results or sync them to Google Sheets.

What to look for in an online quiz maker

Player and response caps

Check free limits against your largest class, not your average one.

Live vs self-paced

Game tools shine live; homework needs a strong self-paced mode.

Auto-grading and feedback

Answer keys, points and instant explanations save hours.

Student access

Codes and links avoid account headaches for younger students.

Data you can use

Per-question results, exports and gradebook sync.

Privacy

Check the vendor's student data privacy terms and your district's approved list (e.g. COPPA/FERPA commitments).
