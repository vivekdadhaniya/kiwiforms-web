Free Form Builder Unlimited Responses: 2026 Picks and the Limits Behind “Unlimited”

Tally, Google Forms, forms.app, Youform and Kiwiform all offer unlimited responses on their free plans, though “unlimited” often carries limits: fair use at Tally, a 5-form cap at forms.app, and paywalled branding removal. These are online form builders, not Apple’s Freeform app. In practice, “unlimited” means no response cap, not that every feature is free.

By the Kiwiform Team · Last updated: October 27, 2026 · Kiwiform publishes this comparison and lists its own product first. Competitor prices come from each vendor’s pricing page as checked on October 24, 2026.

TL;DR

  • Five of the builders compared here put no cap on free responses; the limits table below lists them.
  • If you need many live forms, check the form count too, because forms.app’s free plan caps it.
  • If a capped plan from Jotform, Typeform, Fillout or Microsoft Forms already stopped your form, the alternatives table below shows each cap.
  • Budget for at least one paid feature: branding removal, a custom domain or payments costs money on almost every plan here.
  • For survey-first work, Surveyplanet, SurveyHero and SurveyMars state unlimited questions and responses for free.

Top free form builders with unlimited responses

All five builders in the limits table collect unlimited responses for $0. If you want a free form builder with unlimited forms as well, choose one of the first four, because forms.app caps the number of free forms.

1. Kiwiform

Our product

Kiwiform has no monthly or per-form response cap. Its Free plan includes unlimited forms, unlimited form fields and unlimited responses. The Kiwiform fair-use policy sets no hard submission limit. It only restricts automated flooding, abusive load and prohibited content such as phishing. Tally and Youform also offer unlimited free submissions, so Kiwiform competes on layout, AI drafting and logic depth instead.

Free plan
Unlimited forms, fields and responses, with 1 workspace and 1 team member.
Key features
Conversational one-question-at-a-time and classic layouts are both on Free. Conditional logic, branching, scoring, calculations and multiple endings are on Free. Free also includes native integrations such as Google Sheets, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Slack, Notion, Airtable, Zapier and Make (full Kiwiform integrations list).
AI drafting
Kiwizard AI turns a plain-language prompt into an editable draft that you refine yourself. Free includes 5 Kiwizard AI forms, and Pro makes them unlimited.
Catch
Free allows 20 MB per upload and 1 GB of total storage. Stripe payments, webhooks, custom domains, branding removal, response downloads and partial submissions all require Pro. There are no approval workflows and no PDF editor on any plan.
What adopting it requires
You need a Pro upgrade once you want payments, your own domain or no branding. It is also a young product: v1.0.0 launched on March 1, 2026.
Paid entry price
Pro costs $19 per month, or $14 per month billed yearly, as of October 2026 (Kiwiform pricing). It comes with a 7-day free trial and adds 3 team members, 10 workspaces, 200 MB uploads and 20 GB of storage.

2. Tally

Tally gives unlimited forms and submissions on its free plan, under a fair-use policy. Its editor works like a text document, in a Notion-style layout.

Free plan
Unlimited forms and submissions, subject to fair use.
Key features
Conditional logic, calculations and answer piping. Stripe payments. File uploads up to 10 MB per file, per Tally’s help pages. Native Google Sheets, Notion and Airtable connections. Forms can close on a set date or after a set number of responses.
Catch
Tally’s fair-use guidelines give example thresholds of 50,000 submissions a month, 100 GB of uploads a month or 500 GB stored, and 50,000 emails a month, sustained for 3-4 months. Free forms carry a Tally badge. Removing it, partial submissions and custom domains require Pro. Spiceform’s review reports a commission on payments collected through free forms, so confirm the fee on Tally’s pricing page before you charge.
What adopting it requires
You need to be comfortable with a document-style editor, and you need Pro for a branded address.
Paid entry price
Pro costs $24 per month billed monthly, and yearly billing saves 2 months (as of October 2026).

3. Google Forms

Google Forms offers unlimited forms and unlimited responses to anyone with a Google account. Responses sync natively to Google Sheets.

Free plan
Unlimited forms and responses.
Key features
Native Sheets sync, section branching, file uploads, a one-response-per-user setting, and closing by date, time or response count.
Catch
Design and logic are basic, and there are no native payments. Older posts quote much higher or lower response caps, and those figures are out of date. Uploads count against the owner’s 15 GB of shared Google storage. Respondents must sign in to upload files. Google’s help pages also cap a form at 300 items, 75 sections and 2,000 choices per question.
What adopting it requires
You need a Google account. Calculations have to live in the linked Sheet. Advanced handoffs need Apps Script or third-party automation.
Paid entry price
Google Forms is free at the core, with no paid base plan required (as of October 2026).

4. Youform

Youform gives unlimited forms and responses on its free plan, within fair usage. It is a newer, Typeform-style builder.

Free plan
Unlimited forms and responses, subject to fair usage.
Key features
You can import existing forms from Typeform and Google Forms.
Catch
Branding removal, a custom domain, Stripe payments and partial submissions are all on Pro. Some AI answers list partial submissions as free; Youform’s own page places them on Pro.
What adopting it requires
You need Pro before taking payments or publishing a form without Youform branding.
Paid entry price
Pro costs $20 per month billed annually, or $29 billed monthly (as of October 2026).

5. forms.app

forms.app promises unlimited responses, unlimited views and unlimited team members on its free plan. Its homepage does not claim unlimited forms.

Free plan
Unlimited responses, views and team members. Third-party comparisons from freeformbuilders.com and formbuilder.com put the free form limit at 5.
Key features
Conditional logic, AI drafting, e-signatures, a large template library, and payment collection with no commission (per typeformalternative.com).
Catch
You get 5 free forms, 10 MB uploads and forms.app branding. Paid plans control branding and storage expansion.
What adopting it requires
You have to decide which forms stay active. Running more than five forms means a paid plan.
Paid entry price
Not covered in our October 2026 price check. See forms.app’s pricing page.

Free form builder hidden limits: what “unlimited” leaves out

Free form builders hide limits in the fine print. Even when a plan says “unlimited,” you can still run into response caps, storage ceilings, forced branding and paywalled features. Check these six areas before launch:

Caps and fair use

“Unlimited” often means “unlimited within fair use.” Tally publishes example thresholds, and our own fair-use policy (entry 1) targets abuse rather than volume.

Storage and uploads

A file cap per upload and a total storage pool apply even when responses are uncapped. On Google Forms, uploads consume the owner’s Drive storage.

Branding

Every free plan in the limits table shows the builder’s branding. Removing it is a frequent reason people upgrade.

Paywalled features

Partial submissions, custom domains, webhooks and response exports are often paid. Check each one you plan to use.

Team seats

Many free plans allow one editor. forms.app is the exception, with unlimited team members.

Sign-in and access

Google Forms requires respondents to sign in before they can upload files, which can lower completion rates on public forms.

Are there free form builders with no hidden fees?

Yes, if “no hidden fees” means no response cap and no charge to publish. No, if it means nothing is paywalled. Look for three costs:

  1. 1

    Upgrade fees: You pay to remove branding or add a custom domain on most free plans.

  2. 2

    Payment commissions: Some free plans add a commission on each payment collected, and the rate varies by vendor.

  3. 3

    Overage charges: Some survey tools bill extra responses beyond a plan’s allowance (see SurveyMonkey in the survey section).
