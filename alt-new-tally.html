Tally in 2026: what you get, and what you pay for

Tally's pitch is simple: build forms like you write a doc, for free. It's a strong deal, and it's worth being clear about where its limits actually are.

Plan Listed price What it includes
Free $0 Unlimited forms and submissions (fair use), logic, calculations, answer piping, payments, signatures, file uploads (10 MB/file), webhooks, Sheets/Notion/Airtable/Zapier/Make
Pro $24/mo Remove branding, custom domains, unlimited team members, partial submissions, custom CSS, drop-off analytics, unlimited upload size, 30-day version history
Business $74/mo Data retention controls, email verification, 90-day version history
Source: tally.so/pricing and Tally fair-use policy, September 2026. Tally offers "2 months off" on yearly billing.

The fair-use line: Tally's policy flags accounts that consistently collect 50,000+ submissions a month, send 50,000+ notification emails, or upload around 100 GB of files a month, and then offers a custom plan. For most users that's effectively unlimited.

Why people look for a Tally alternative

1. Long pages hurt completion

Tally's document layout works for short forms. For longer lead, application or feedback forms, a one-question-per-screen flow usually feels shorter to respondents.

2. Design needs CSS

Deeper styling on Tally depends on Pro's custom CSS. Teams without a developer want visual theme controls.

3. No AI builder

Tally doesn't generate forms from a prompt, and reviewers report its founders have said AI and enterprise features aren't on the roadmap.

4. Analytics are Pro

Drop-off analytics and extended visit data are Pro features, and there's no built-in response analysis.

5. Marketing-stack depth

Teams running lead generation often want CRM and email tools (HubSpot, Klaviyo, Mailchimp, GoHighLevel) connected natively instead of through Zapier.

6. Quizzes and outcomes

Scored quizzes with different endings per result are easier in tools built around scoring and outcome logic.

1. Kiwiform : Tally's freedom, in a conversational format

Our pick Best free Tally alternative

If you picked Tally because it's free and unlimited, Kiwiform keeps that: unlimited forms, unlimited responses and conditional logic on the free plan. The difference is the experience. Kiwiform guides respondents through one question at a time, which suits lead-generation, application, onboarding and feedback forms where a long page feels like work.

You can build visually with drag-and-drop, or describe the form to the AI form builder and edit the draft. The logic tools are free: conditional logic , recall, branching paths, show/hide fields, scoring and calculations, custom variables, quiz variables and multiple endings.

Fillout is the natural step for teams whose data lives in Notion, Airtable or HubSpot. It includes unlimited seats on every plan, and the free plan covers 1,000 responses a month with logic, payments, scheduling and PDF generation.

3. Typeform: premium conversational forms

Typeform is the original conversational form builder, with polished design and a mature ecosystem. The catch is pricing by response: Basic ($28/mo listed) includes 100 responses a month and 1 user, and branding removal starts on Plus ($56/mo, 1,000 responses).

  • Pros
  • Best-in-class design
  • Large integration ecosystem
  • Cons
  • Response caps and higher cost
  • HIPAA, SSO and custom domain are Enterprise-only

Verdict:great if budget isn't a concern. For the same style without caps, see our Typeform alternatives guide.

4. Jotform: operations, approvals & HIPAA

Jotform is the heavyweight: approvals, PDFs, e-signatures, payment gateways, tables and thousands of templates, with HIPAA options on selected paid plans. You'll trade Tally's simplicity for a busier interface and per-plan form and submission limits.

Free plan5 forms · 100 submissions/mo

5. Paperform: doc-style, but design-first

If you like Tally's doc-style editing but want richer, landing-page-like design, bookings and products, Paperform is the closest match. The free plan allows 30 submissions a month, and paid plans remove submission caps.

Free plan30 submissions/mo
PaidPro $49/mo annual ($59 monthly) · Business $124/mo annual

Verdict: choose it when presentation matters most and budget allows.

6. Formbricks: open-source & self-hosted

Formbricks is open-source survey software for link, website and in-product surveys. Self-host it, or use the cloud: Hobby is free for 250 responses a month, Pro is $74/month (2,000 responses) and Scale is $325/month.

Verdict: the pick if data ownership or self-hosting is a requirement.

7. Google Forms: free and familiar

Google Forms is free, unlimited and native to Sheets, which is fine for internal surveys and quizzes. Compared with Tally you lose design flexibility, payments and modern embeds.

Verdict: internal use only. Google Forms alternatives Kiwiform for Google Sheets

8. Zoho Forms: for the Zoho ecosystem

A value pick if you run Zoho CRM. The free plan includes 3 forms and 500 submissions a month, and paid tiers raise limits substantially (Basic includes 10,000 submissions a month).

9. Microsoft Forms: for Microsoft 365 teams

Microsoft Forms is included with Microsoft 365 and works well inside Teams and Excel. It's basic for customer-facing forms and doesn't collect payments. Microsoft Forms alternatives

How to switch from Typeform to Kiwiform in about 5 minutes

1

Export submissions In Tally, download your submissions as CSV so you keep your history.

2

Rebuild with AI . Paste your Tally questions into the AI form builder or start from a template. Long Tally pages become a guided flow automatically.

3

Recreate logic. Map Tally's conditional blocks to Kiwiform's branching, recall and show/hide rules, then add scoring or multiple endings if useful.

4

Style it. Set fonts, colors and buttons visually, with no CSS needed.

5

Reconnect tools. Google Sheets, Notion, Airtable, Slack, Zapier or Make, and HubSpot, Klaviyo or Mailchimp for leads.

6

Replace the link or embed on your site, then close the Tally form.
