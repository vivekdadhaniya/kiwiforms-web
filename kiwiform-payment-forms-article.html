6 Best Tools for Collecting Payments Through Forms in 2026

You've got the form live. Registrations are coming in, the donation page looks good, or your order form is already embedded on your site. Then comes the hard part: the payment step. That's where people drop off if checkout feels bolted on instead of built in.

A good payment form builder lets you collect payments online inside the form itself, with no redirect, no second tool and no manual follow-up. This guide compares the 6 best tools for collecting payments through forms on what actually matters: payment gateways, fees, recurring billing, pricing logic and free-plan limits.

Last Updated: October 13, 2026

Quick answer

The best tools for collecting payments through forms:

  • Best free option with native Stripe: Kiwiform.
  • Most payment gateways: Jotform.
  • Best conversational checkout: Typeform.
  • Best for products and subscriptions: Paperform.
  • Best for unlimited payments and calculations: Cognito Forms.
  • Best for enterprise and regulated teams: Formstack Forms.

Pick based on the payment processor you must support, whether you need recurring billing, and your monthly budget.

What to look for in a payment form builder

Before comparing tools, check five things:

01

Gateway support

Make sure the tool supports the processor you already use, such as Stripe, PayPal or Square.

02

Fees on top of processing

Some builders add their own commission on top of Stripe or PayPal fees.

03

Recurring billing

Subscriptions, memberships and installment plans need native recurring payments.

04

Pricing logic

Calculated totals, quantities, coupons and conditional pricing reduce manual work.

05

Security

The payment step should run through a PCI-compliant processor so card details never touch your form data.

2. Jotform

Best for: businesses that need a specific or regional payment gateway

Jotform connects to more than 40 payment processors, more than most form builders. If your accounting team already uses a particular gateway, or a client contract requires one, that breadth saves you from rebuilding your stack later.

Best for processor flexibility and payment logic

Jotform supports recurring billing, variable pricing based on answers, coupons and product inventory. That makes it strong for paid registrations, service packages and order forms. Its large template library and workflow automation help once the first form works.

Trade-offs: The breadth can feel heavy if you only need a simple donation form. The free plan has form and submission limits, so costs can rise as volume grows.

  • Pros
  • Widest gateway coverage
  • Subscriptions
  • Inventory
  • Templates
  • Cons
  • Busy builder
  • Submission caps

3. Typeform

Best for: brand-forward registrations, donations and simple paid requests

Typeform's Payment question connects to Stripe or PayPal, so the payment step sits inside a conversational, one-question-at-a-time flow. You need a Basic or higher plan to use payments.

When the UX matters as much as the transaction

The one-question format makes a paid flow feel less intimidating. Typeform also supports calculated prices, including "pay what you want" donation amounts.

Trade-offs: It's not built for complex ecommerce, product catalogs or multi-item carts. Stripe users in India can't take payments through Typeform's Payment question, according to Typeform's help center.

  • Pros
  • Polished design
  • Easy setup
  • Stripe or PayPal
  • Cons
  • Paid plan required
  • Limited ecommerce features

4. Paperform

Best for: selling products, bookings and subscriptions from a form that looks like a landing page

Paperform supports Stripe, PayPal, Square, Braintree and Google Pay. Its ecommerce features include subscriptions, coupons, tax, product management and inventory, which makes it one of the best fits for selling directly through a form.

Strong for products, bundles and bookings

For coaching, digital products, event tickets or service bookings, Paperform can replace a separate cart. Calculated fields help when pricing depends on selections or quantities. Building order forms for ecommerce? Compare both tools first.

Trade-offs: There's more setup than a simple contact-to-pay form needs, and payment features are mainly on paid plans.

  • Pros
  • Five gateways
  • Subscriptions
  • Coupons
  • Tax
  • Cons
  • Learning curve
  • Paid plans for payments

5. Cognito Forms

Best for: event tickets, registrations and forms with calculated totals

Cognito Forms supports Stripe, PayPal and Square natively and advertises unlimited payments on every plan. That makes budgeting easier when payment collection is a core part of your workflow.

Good for event tickets and clean budget planning

Cognito handles product lists, quantities, inventory and dynamic totals, so you can build tiered ticketing and add-ons for event registration forms without a separate cart. Card-on-file options through supported gateways help with follow-up charges. It also works well for donation forms for nonprofits.

Trade-offs: Recurring billing is handled through the gateway rather than in the form. Some plans add a small Cognito fee on top of gateway fees, so check the terms for your region.

  • Pros
  • Unlimited payments
  • Strong calculations
  • Three gateways
  • Cons
  • Plainer design
  • Possible extra fee

6. Formstack Forms

Best for: enterprise, healthcare and Salesforce-based teams

Formstack supports Stripe, PayPal Pro, Authorize.net and other processors, with recurring payments on select gateways. It's built for teams where the payment form is part of a larger compliance or CRM process.

Built for regulated teams and complex handoffs

Salesforce teams, healthcare organizations and regulated industries care most about whether data lands in the right system with the right controls. Formstack's logic, templates and documentation reduce setup risk there.

Trade-offs: There's no free plan, and pricing is higher than most SMB tools. Some gateway setups need extra configuration.

  • Pros
  • Enterprise integrations
  • Multiple gateways
  • Compliance focus
  • Cons
  • Expensive
  • No free plan

Honorable mentions

Tally

A simple, Notion-style builder with Stripe payments on the free plan. Tally charges a 5% commission per payment on the free plan, removed on Pro, and doesn't support recurring payments.

Zoho Forms

Lets you drag in a payment gateway and use formula fields for discounts and totals. A good fit if you already use Zoho apps.

Google Forms

Has no native payment collection. You can add a Stripe or PayPal payment link to the form, but you can't track who paid inside Google Forms. See our Google Forms alternative page.

Fees to know before you choose

Every payment form has two possible fee layers:

Layer 1

Processor fees

Stripe, PayPal and Square charge per transaction. You pay these with any form builder.

Layer 2

Form builder fees

Some tools add their own commission, especially on free plans. Tally's free plan is one example (5%).

Always check both layers. A "free" form builder with a 5% commission can cost more than a paid plan once you process real volume.

How to collect payments through a form (step by step)

  1. 1

    Create a Stripe account (or the gateway your tool supports) and verify your business details.

  2. 2

    Build your form with the fields you need, such as name, email, ticket type and quantity.

  3. 3

    Add a payment field and connect it to your Stripe account.

  4. 4

    Set the price, either fixed or calculated from answers using logic and variables.

  5. 5

    Turn on Apple Pay and Google Pay in Stripe for faster mobile checkout.

  6. 6

    Test with a small live payment, then refund it.

  7. 7

    Publish or embed the form on your site and send confirmation emails after payment.
